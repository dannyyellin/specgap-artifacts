Comparison: Revised Results vs Initial Results — RESTestBench
==================================================================
Date: 2026-05-26

Summary of changes between initial and revised experiments:
  - Prompts: revised terminology (properties, not behaviors), symmetric
    output schema, new categories.txt with examples, CODEGEN_REQUIREMENTS
    added to detector spec, "domain-standard behaviors" removed as valid
    omission source
  - Methods: 4 detection methods (zeroshot, oneshot-tracker, oneshot-warehouse,
    twoshot) vs. single zeroshot in initial experiments
  - Model: gpt-5.4 throughout (initial used gpt-4.1-mini for code gen on
    some runs; gpt-5.4 added as comparison in initial)
  - RealWorld Round 1: R20 replaced with R23 (R20 conflicted with slug
    code-gen instruction; R23 creates a genuine gap)

EFFECT OF EXAMPLES (FEW-SHOT) ACROSS EXPERIMENTS
==================================================================

The value of in-context examples differs markedly between the two
rounds of experiments:

  Initial experiments (gpt-4.1-mini, microservices):

    Method              Recall
    Zero-shot           10/20 (50%)
    One-shot (tracker)  10/20 (50%)
    One-shot (warehouse)  9/20 (45%)
    Two-shot             7/20 (35%)
    Union of all 3       15/20 (75%)

    Each variant found UNIQUE gaps the others missed. The examples
    acted as "attention primers" that directed the model toward
    different gap types:
      - Tracker example → validation gaps, auth gaps
      - Warehouse example → field-update gaps, business-logic guards
    Running the union of 3 prompts raised recall from 50% to 75%.

  Revised experiments (gpt-5.4, RESTestBench):

    Method              RealWorld  FastAPI  TodoApp
    Zero-shot           13/16      9/13     2/8
    One-shot (tracker)  12/16      9/13     2/8
    One-shot (warehouse)12/16      8/13     2/8
    Two-shot            12/16      9/13     2/8
    Union of all 4      13/16      9/13     2/8

    Zero-shot is the strongest method (or tied). The example-based
    methods add noise without adding unique detections. The union of
    all 4 methods matches zero-shot alone.

  Interpretation: gpt-5.4 already reasons about all gap categories
  (ENDPOINTS, validations, field-update logic, business rules) from
  zero-shot. The attention-priming effect that helped gpt-4.1-mini is
  no longer needed — gpt-5.4 covers these patterns without examples.

  This is consistent with the general finding that stronger models
  benefit less from few-shot demonstrations because they are better
  zero-shot reasoners.

  CAVEAT: Two confounds prevent a clean conclusion:
  (a) The prompts changed between rounds (not just the model).
      The revised zero-shot prompt is better structured than the
      initial one, which may close part of the gap.
  (b) The datasets differ (microservices vs. RESTestBench).
      It is possible the RESTestBench gaps are simpler and don't
      require the attention-priming that microservice gaps do.
  Holding all other variables constant by re-running the initial
  microservice experiments with gpt-5.4 would isolate the model
  effect cleanly. See TODO.

PRECISION COMPARISON
==================================================================

  Initial (gpt-4.1-mini, single zeroshot):
    FastAPI R1:  13 additions + 11 omissions = 24 total, ~2 TP → ~8%
    FastAPI R2:   5 additions +  9 omissions = 14 total, ~6 TP → ~43%
    TodoApp:      7 additions +  2 omissions =  9 total,  2 TP → ~22%
    RealWorld R1: 4 additions +  4 omissions =  8 total, ~4 TP → ~50%
    RealWorld R2: 7 additions +  7 omissions = 14 total, ~4 TP → ~29%
    Average: ~30%

  Revised (gpt-5.4, zeroshot):
    FastAPI R1:   4 additions +  4 omissions =  8 total, ~4 TP → ~50%
    FastAPI R2:   3 additions +  7 omissions = 10 total, ~7 TP → ~70%
    TodoApp:      5 additions +  2 omissions =  7 total,  2 TP → ~29%
    RealWorld R1: 5 additions + 13 omissions = 18 total, ~9 TP → ~50%
    RealWorld R2: 6 additions +  7 omissions = 13 total, ~5 TP → ~38%
    Average: ~47%

  Precision roughly doubles from initial to revised. Fewer false-positive
  omissions (no spurious domain-standard behavior claims) and more focused
  additions account for most of the improvement.
